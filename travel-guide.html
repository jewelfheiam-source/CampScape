Travel Information & Tourist Guide

Everything to know before you pitch a tent in Oriental Mindoro.

When to Visit

Best months: December to May, the dry season, with calm seas for island hopping and clear nights for stargazing. June to November brings the southwest monsoon and typhoon risk; coastal camping is often suspended and ferries are frequently cancelled, so always check the weather and Coast Guard advisories before sailing.

Weather Notes

Daytime temperatures run around 28 to 33 degrees Celsius on the coast, cooler in the mountains. Sea breezes drop at night; even in summer, bring a light layer for 2 AM campfire duty. Mountain camps on the Halcon range can dip below 10 degrees Celsius.

What to Bring

  • Tent with rainfly, groundsheet, and stakes (or reserve a rental)
  • Sleeping bag or light blanket; inflatable mat
  • Headlamp or flashlight plus spare batteries
  • Power bank, dry bags, and ziplock bags for gadgets
  • Quick-dry clothing, swimwear, rash guard, cap, sandals plus trek shoes
  • Reef-safe sunscreen, insect repellent, personal meds
  • Water bottles (at least 2 liters per day) and purification tablets
  • Cash in small bills; ATMs are scarce outside Calapan, Puerto Galera, and Roxas
  • Trash bags: pack out everything you pack in

Transportation Tips

From Manila, buses to Batangas Port leave from Buendia (Gil Puyat) and PITX, taking 2 to 3 hours. Ferries to Calapan run nearly round the clock; Puerto Galera sailings run mostly daytime, so arrive at Batangas Port before mid-afternoon. If you miss the last Puerto Galera ferry, route via Calapan and ride a van south, about 80 minutes. In-province transport is by jeepney, tricycle, and van shuttles; island transfers are by rented banca.

Camping Safety and Emergencies

  • Tell your campsite host your itinerary and expected return.
  • Camp above the high-tide line and never on dunes.
  • No bonfires during windy or dry-banner days; use designated fire pits only.
  • Watch sea conditions: jellyfish season and riptides vary by beach.
  • Save emergency numbers: 911 national hotline; municipal tourism offices assist with rescue coordination.

Travel Responsibly

Local Customs and Responsible Tourism

Respect Communities

Greet with "good morning/po at salamat," dress modestly in villages and at church areas, and always ask before photographing people, especially members of the Mangyan indigenous communities.

Follow Campsite Rules

Quiet hours are usually 10 PM; keep tents within marked areas; never dig or cut vegetation; and settle fees honestly at registration.

Protect the Wild

Do not touch corals or chase marine life, never feed wildlife, stay on trails, and take all trash back out with you. Leave every campsite better than you found it.
