Camping Destinations & Tourism Attractions

Real places, honest estimates, and everything you need to pick your next campsite in Oriental Mindoro.

Accuracy note: Destination details are drawn from the official provincial tourism guide and municipal sources. Entrance and camping fees marked estimated have not been verified with the site operator and may change; always confirm before travel.

Beach & Island

Coastal Camping

White Beach, Puerto Galera

Barangay San Isidro, Puerto Galera · 130 km from Manila

Oriental Mindoro's most famous beach strip: fine white sand, calm swimming, beachfront resorts, and a long row of restaurants and bars that light up at sunset. Several resorts offer beachfront tent rentals alongside rooms, and public shore areas are used by groups for overnight beach camping with owner permission.

SwimmingSnorkelingSunsetBeach tents (resort-run)

Facilities: Cottages, restrooms, restaurants, dive shops, ATMs nearby, boat rental.

Estimated fees: Environmental user fee ₱50 (verified via port sources); resort tent rental from about ₱500 to ₱1,500 per night (estimated).

Getting there: Bus from Manila (Buendia or PITX, around ₱300) to Batangas Port, then fastcraft or RoRo ferry to Balatero or Muelle Pier, Puerto Galera (45 min to 1 hr 15 min). Tricycle to White Beach.

Sabang Beach, Puerto Galera

East of Puerto Galera proper · 15 min by jeepney or banca from the pier

Sabang is diving central, gateway to the coral gardens and the famous wreck and reef dive sites, with a compact strip of inns, dive centers, and budget eateries. Quieter coves nearby suit small groups who want to camp close to the action.

DivingNightlifeDiver's pick

Facilities: Dive shops, guesthouses, restaurants, ferry connections to Batangas.

Estimated fees: No general beach fee; dive packages from about ₱1,800 per dive trip (estimated).

Getting there: Direct Batangas to Sabang ferry, or via Muelle/Balatero then jeepney.

Bulalacao Island Circuit (Suguicay, Aslom, Buyayao)

Southernmost municipality of Oriental Mindoro

The province's hidden island paradise. Suguicay, Aslom, and Buyayao islands offer powdery sand, clear shallows, and genuinely quiet nights. Balatasan Long Beach nearby has free admission according to the provincial tourism listing; huts and floating cottages are available, and it serves as a jump-off to Tambaron Island.

Island hoppingBeach campingUncrowded

Facilities: Basic cottages and huts; limited signal; bring your own camping gear and water.

Estimated fees: Balatasan Long Beach free admission (per provincial tourism guide); island fees and boat rental to be confirmed locally (estimated ₱1,500 to ₱3,000 per boat per day).

Getting there: Bus from Batangas or Calapan to Bulalacao town proper, then San Jose-bound jeepney to the jump-off barangay, or RoRo ferry from Batangas to Roxas then bus south.

Banilad Beach, Pinamalayan

Barangay Bongol, Pinamalayan · about 2 hours from town proper by jeepney and tricycle

A secluded two-kilometer white-sand beach that stays blissfully quiet even in summer, ideal for families and small groups who want the shore to themselves.

QuietSwimmingOff the beaten path

Facilities: Limited; day cottages available; confirm camping permission with barangay or beach caretakers.

Estimated fees: To be confirmed locally (estimated ₱20 to ₱50 entrance).

Getting there: Bongol-bound jeepney from Pinamalayan town proper, then tricycle to the beach.

Lake, Falls & Mountains

Inland and Highland Adventures

Lake Naujan National Park

Naujan municipality · one of the country's national parks

The fifth-largest lake in the Philippines and a protected national park, a haven for birdwatchers with resident and migratory waterbirds. Lakeside shorelines and nearby resorts suit quiet tent camping and early-morning paddles.

BirdwatchingKayakingNature retreat

Facilities: Park viewing areas; basic resorts nearby; bring camping gear.

Estimated fees: To be confirmed with the park office (estimated under ₱100).

Getting there: Jeepney from Calapan City toward Naujan, around 30 to 45 minutes.

Book / Inquire

Tamaraw Falls, Puerto Galera

Barangay Villa Cagbalinan Road, Puerto Galera

The province's most photographed waterfall: twin cascades pouring beside a road-side bridge, with natural cold-spring pools below where you can cool off after the ride from Calapan.

WaterfallSwimmingRoad-trip stop

Facilities: Cottages and sari-sari stores near the falls; day-visit site (no camping).

Estimated fees: To be confirmed locally (estimated ₱30 to ₱100).

Getting there: 1 to 1.5 hours by van or jeepney from Calapan toward Puerto Galera; stop is along the main road.

Mount Halcon (for experienced mountaineers)

Baco municipality · among the highest peaks in the Philippines

The legendary giant of Mindoro, famous among Filipino mountaineers for its rainforest, mossy forest, and demanding multi-day climbs. Climbs require permits and guides arranged in advance through local authorities; this is a trek-and-camp expedition, not a casual weekend.

Major climbPermit requiredExpert level

Facilities: None on the mountain; full expedition gear, guide, and porter arrangements needed.

Estimated fees: Guide and permit costs to be confirmed with Baco LGU (estimated ₱1,500 to ₱3,500 per group).

Getting there: Calapan City, then Baco municipality; registration at the designated jump-off.

Inquire about permits

Tukuran Falls and Lagoon, Puerto Galera

Barangay Baclayan, inland Puerto Galera

A two-tier waterfall with a tranquil lagoon reachable by a short hike or carabao-drawn cart ride, a favorite half-day nature escape for campers based at Puerto Galera beaches.

WaterfallNature walkFamily-friendly

Facilities: Local guide required for the trail; small store at the entrance.

Estimated fees: Guide fee around ₱300 per group (estimated).

Getting there: From Puerto Galera town proper by tricycle to the trailhead.
